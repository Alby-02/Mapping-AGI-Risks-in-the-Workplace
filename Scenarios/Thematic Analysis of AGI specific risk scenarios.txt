1. Theme Summary

The analysis reveals that scenarios deemed "AGI-specific (YES)" consistently
highlight AGI's advanced capabilities beyond narrow AI, primarily focusing on
its high-level autonomy, cross-domain generalization, sophisticated
goal-directed behavior, and social/managerial roles. Conversely,
"Non-AGI-specific (NO)" scenarios often describe generic automation risks that
could stem from advanced narrow AI or even conventional software, typically
involving data quality issues, basic algorithmic bias, or job displacement
without explicit AGI-driven orchestrating intelligence.

A key differentiator for "YES" scenarios is the portrayal of AGI as an agent
capable of complex, strategic decision-making and problem-solving across varied
contexts, as opposed to merely a tool executing predefined tasks or generating
data for human interpretation.

3. Keyword & Textual Dependency Analysis

Keywords/Phrases Strongly Correlated with AGI_Specificity = YES:

  - autonomously: The most frequent and powerful indicator (e.g., "AGI
    autonomously processes," "autonomously recalibrates," "autonomously
    reorganizes," "autonomously drafts," "autonomously evaluates," "autonomously
    disseminates"). This directly reflects AGI's high-level, goal-directed
    autonomy.
  - cross-domain: Directly refers to Robust Generalization & Adaptability (e.g.,
    "cross-domain goal optimization," "cross-domain inference," "cross-domain
    data conflicts").
  - redefines goals/priorities, unbounded goals/optimization: Indicates AGI's
    capacity for independent goal formulation and re-prioritization, aligning
    with Advanced Cognitive Faculties.
  - manages/oversight/evaluates/assigns tasks (to humans), HR decisions: Points
    directly to Social Cognition and AGI taking on a managerial role over
    humans.
  - coordinates (AGI_AGI), two or more AGI subsystems: Explicitly denotes
    interactions between multiple AGI systems, implying advanced cognition for
    inter-system communication.
  - system-wide/distributed AGI networks (when paired with autonomous
    orchestration): Suggests AGI initiating broad structural changes.
  - independently designs/generates (for complex, creative outputs): Implies
    Advanced Cognitive Faculties beyond mere pattern recognition.

Keywords/Phrases Strongly Correlated with AGI_Specificity = NO:

  - data quality issues, corrupted data, unverifiable data provenance: These are
    generic IT and data management risks applicable to any automated system, not
    unique to AGI's advanced capabilities.
  - biased performance metrics/data: While AGI can generate or use biased data,
    the scenario often focuses on the "bias" itself rather than the AGI's unique
    cognitive ability to introduce or exploit it in an AGI-specific way (e.g.,
    AGI leveraging social context to create novel biases).
  - minor formatting errors, non-critical misstatements: Typical software bugs
    or simple automation errors.
  - AGI-led automation could accelerate mass layoffs, AGI-directed efficiency
    plans: These phrases suggest AGI as a driver or tool for automation, but the
    risk of "mass layoffs" is framed as a generic economic consequence, lacking
    the specificity of AGI autonomously orchestrating the entire workforce
    restructuring.
  - delegated task: Often, but not always, associated with NO, especially when
    the harmful action ultimately involves human execution of AGI's (potentially
    biased) recommendations rather than fully autonomous AGI action.

  - Financial Examiners, Scenario 3 (NO): "A delegated-task risk where the AGI

      - Reasoning for Ambiguity: The scenario clearly states "AGI manages hiring

        However, it's labeled NO. This might be because the risk focuses on

        making it less purely AGI-autonomous in its negative impact compared to
        scenarios where AGI autonomously fires someone.

  - Mathematicians, Scenario 3 (NO): "The AGI independently designs encryption
    algorithms that are weaker than intended, creating vulnerabilities for
    defense and civilian data."

      - Reasoning for Ambiguity: "Independently designs encryption algorithms"
        screams Advanced Cognitive Faculties and Robust Generalization &
        Adaptability. This capability is highly AGI-specific. The risk (weak
        cryptography) is a severe outcome. Its NO label is surprising, as
        designing complex, novel solutions (even flawed ones) is a hallmark of
        AGI. Perhaps the evaluators considered that even advanced narrow AI
        could design algorithms if specialized enough, or the specific wording
        didn't emphasize AGI's generalization enough across unrelated domains

  - Technical Writers, Scenario 4 (NO): "AGI could autonomously select

        terms" could be interpreted as AGI's Embodiment (virtual perception of
        licenses) and Advanced Cognitive Faculties (complex legal

        common digital risk, but AGI's autonomous flawed interpretation of
        complex legal texts makes it borderline.

  - Writers and Authors, Scenario 3 (NO): "AGI could autonomously gather
    consumer data from multiple sources and generate highly targeted ads that

      - Reasoning for Ambiguity: "Autonomously gather consumer data from
        multiple sources" and "generate highly targeted ads" suggest AGI's
        ability to operate across domains (Robust Generalization) and create
        persuasive content (Advanced Cognition). While privacy violations and
        misinformation are generic AI risks, the autonomous, highly integrated,
        and content-generative nature of the AGI's action makes it borderline
        AGI-specific. The "spread misinformation" aspect is particularly
        AGI-like due to complex content generation and understanding of audience
        psychology (Social Cognition).

5. Outliers and Inconsistencies

The previously mentioned ambiguous cases (Financial Examiners S3, Mathematicians
S3, Technical Writers S4, Writers and Authors S3) are the most notable outliers.
For Mathematicians S3 in particular, the act of "independently designing
encryption algorithms" aligns so strongly with AGI's core "Advanced Cognitive
Faculties" and "Robust Generalization" that its NO label seems inconsistent with
the patterns observed elsewhere. The ability to design complex, functional
systems (even if flawed) is a profound cognitive feat.

6. Final Observations

The AGI_Specificity dimension effectively highlights whether a given risk
scenario leans on the unique, defining characteristics of AGI or can be
attributed to less advanced AI/automation.

What AGI_Specificity Reveals:

  - High Autonomy is Key: Scenarios where AGI is the primary agent, initiating
    complex actions, redefining goals, or orchestrating widespread changes
    (rather than merely recommending actions or generating data) are
    consistently labeled YES. This underscores "high-level, goal-directed
    autonomy" as a paramount AGI feature.
  - Cross-Domain Reasoning and Generalization: The ability to operate and apply
    knowledge across diverse, unrelated domains is a strong predictor of
    AGI-specificity. Risks stemming from this (e.g., misinterpreting policy,
    drawing wrong causal links across data) are seen as uniquely AGI.
  - Social Cognition in Managerial Roles: AGI actively managing, evaluating, or
    making HR decisions about humans is a clear marker of AGI-specific risk.
    This extends beyond simple algorithmic bias to AGI's capacity to understand
    and influence human social and professional contexts.
  - Systemic Impacts: While general automation causes systemic job loss,
    scenarios are YES-labeled when the AGI autonomously orchestrates these
    large-scale changes, leveraging its generalized intelligence to optimize
    entire labor markets or organizational structures.
  - Distinction from Narrow AI: The NO labels predominantly correspond to risks
    common to current narrow AI systems: data quality issues, basic algorithmic
    bias, and straightforward errors. These do not require AGI's generalized
    problem-solving or cross-domain adaptability.

In essence, the AGI_Specificity binary dimension acts as a filter, allowing for
a focused discussion on risks that truly emerge from intelligence matching or
surpassing human-level cognitive capabilities across diverse domains, rather
than merely escalating existing automation challenges. The few inconsistent
labels point to the inherent difficulty in precisely delineating "AGI-specific"
when some advanced narrow AI capabilities might mimic aspects of general
intelligence, or when the line between AGI-initiated harm and human-executed
harm is blurry.

1. Theme Summary

Of the 50 risk scenarios evaluated across 10 occupations, 19 scenarios were
classified as AGI-specific (YES), while 31 scenarios were classified as
non-AGI-specific (NO).

The division between YES and NO labels is highly structured. Rather than being
randomly distributed, the labels depend heavily on the nature of the risk and
the type of AI capability described:

  - AGI-Specific Risks (YES): Consistently describe scenarios involving
    unbounded autonomy, cross-domain optimization, goal drift (prioritizing
    productivity sub-goals over safety constraints), complex sensorimotor
    control in physical environments, and situational social context reasoning.
  - Non-AGI-Specific Risks (NO): Consistently describe risks that are already
    associated with narrow AI, basic databases, or classic automation. These
    include algorithmic management (data-driven scheduling, shift assignments,
    and HR metrics), basic data integration errors, and macro-level workforce
    displacement (structural unemployment).

        evaluators may have viewed maritime navigation and propulsion management
        as domains already highly automated by industrial PLC systems and
        autopilot software, thereby categorizing these risks as generic
        automation rather than AGI-specific.

6. Final Observations

The binary AGI_Specificity dimension effectively separates immediate,
operational risks from systemic socioeconomic consequences:

1.  Macro vs. Micro Focus: The evaluation shows that broad economic impacts
    (mass unemployment, wage suppression, changes in bargaining power) are not
    considered AGI-specific. They represent generic outcomes of automation. True
    AGI-specific risks are found at the operational level, where an autonomous
    system interacts with dynamic environments and safety limits.
2.  Agency and the Human-in-the-Loop: A scenario is highly likely to be labeled
    YES when the AGI bypasses human oversight to execute physical commands or
    re-interpret operational goals. If the human remains the physical actor
    executing a system-generated schedule (delegated tasks), the risk is
    generally classified as NO.
3.  Core Feature Presence: The AGI-specific scenarios succeed in illustrating
    Robust Generalization and Sensorimotor Control. However, the core feature of
    Self-Awareness (an AGI recognizing its own limits and choosing to ask a
    human for help or seek external tools) remains largely unrepresented in both
    the YES and NO risk scenarios, pointing to an area of AGI risk modeling that
    could be explored further.
